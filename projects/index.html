Cullergrader is an open-source Java desktop app and CLI tool that groups similar photos using a perceptual hash for efficient similarity comparisons.

I built it to solve my own photography problem: taking multiple photos of the same shot for the best take means you'll have to sort through thousands of photos afterwards.

Released on macOS, Windows, and Linux -- feel free to contribute :)

FaunaDex performs semantic species retrieval on animal photos by matching visual descriptions with a vector search. Instead of training on individual photos of each species, the database can be expanded simply by adding more embeddings.

It supports 9,641 species, 4.7x more than Google SpeciesNet's 2,066. I've also published the dataset on Hugging Face, where it has 100+ downloads.

Goodminton automatically tracks your badminton game from your phone by simulating it's own version of your court and birdie.

It combines depth estimation, motion isolation, trajectory modeling (supported by your phone's gyroscope to check for gravity), and iPhone LiDAR for reliable position tracking, and uses a CV model to determine the court's boundaries.

🏸 Goodminton

Pickle is an emergency response app that keeps you and your loved ones safe amidst disaster without the need for an internet connection.

Through Twilio, Pickle shares your location and status during emergencies purely through SMS messages. It also feeds anonymous data into a heatmap for disaster responders, and offers a Cohere-powered survival assistant over text.

🥒 Pickle

Nimbus puts the power of precise drone flight into the hands (or lack thereof, it's hands-free!) of anyone by combining voice and camera input to convert what you see into precise drone actions.

When given a command, it leverages Freesolo's LLM fine-tuning to handle contextual prompts like: "fly to the pillar in the distance" and convert verbal instructions into drone actions.

🚁 Nimbus

Quantum Key Distribution is an encryption method used by governments around the world to securely transmit data by encoding it in the superposition of polarized light.

This project recreates that system by rotating motorized half/quarter-waveplates and polarizers to send bits in a manner that destroys the data if intercepted.
